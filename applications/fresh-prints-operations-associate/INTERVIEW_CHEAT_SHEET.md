# Katuva Interview Cheat Sheet (Natural & Human)

> **Interview Time:** 2:00 PM PHT  
> **Company:** Katuva (Boise, Idaho) – founded by Tobe Brockner  
> **Role:** Marketing Coordinator for a US Wine & Hospitality brand  

---

## ⚡ The 4 Golden Pillars (Katuva's DNA)
1. **SOPs (Standard Operating Procedures):** "I don't just do a task once—I write down the steps into an SOP so anyone on the team can repeat it without confusion."
2. **Extreme Ownership:** "As a business co-owner, I take 100% accountability. If something breaks or runs late, I fix the issue and communicate right away—no excuses."
3. **Human-First AI:** "I use AI like ChatGPT and Claude to speed up brainstorming and remove tedious work, but the authentic human voice and brand storytelling always come from me."
4. **Beverage & Hospitality Storytelling:** "Marketing food and drinks is special—it's about craft, local community, and making people feel welcome. I already live this every day at Morpho Cafe."

---

## 🗣️ How to Answer Naturally (Speak like this)

### 1. "Tell me a bit about yourself and your experience."
> "Sure! To give you the quick story—I actually co-own a local cafe called Morpho Cafe, where I handle both tech and marketing. When we first opened, we needed customers fast, so I jumped in and did everything: shooting videos, editing reels on CapCut, designing posts on Canva, and building our online presence. That actually helped bump our delivery orders by around 30%.  
>  
> From there, I started taking on freelance work for international clients in Canada, the UAE, and Italy—mostly editing short-form videos and creating social assets. I'm also really into AI tools like ChatGPT, which I use daily to draft copy and brainstorm faster. So my background is a mix of hands-on creative work and the practical, day-to-day operations of running a business."

---

### 2. "Why are you interested in this specific role with our wine client?"
> "Honestly, because it's in the food and beverage space. Running a cafe taught me that you're never just selling a drink—you're selling an experience, a craft, and a community feeling. When I saw this was for a wine retail and hospitality company, it just made total sense to me. I already understand that customer mindset, and I want to bring what I've learned running our cafe's marketing to a brand like this."

---

### 3. "What does your video editing and Canva workflow actually look like?"
> "Very practical. With **CapCut**, I take raw footage or event clips, cut out all the awkward pauses and dead air, add clean captions, B-roll, and find music that fits the vibe. I keep it natural and easy to watch.  
>  
> With **Canva**, I focus on building reusable templates for carousels, announcements, and promos so the brand looks consistent and we aren't reinventing the wheel every single time we post."

---

### 4. "We use Klaviyo and Zoho. Have you used those before?"
*(Be honest, then pivot to how fast you learn systems)*
> "To be completely transparent, for our cafe we haven't done deep email marketing in Klaviyo yet—most of our customer communication happens on social media and our ordering apps. But I'm very comfortable with tech and workflows. I understand the strategy behind email: why someone opens an email, how follow-up sequences work, and how to segment customers. If I need to get familiar with the buttons inside Klaviyo or Zoho, I can pick that up in a weekend."

---

### 5. "How do you actually use AI in your day-to-day work?"
> "I don't use AI to spit out generic robot text. I mostly treat ChatGPT or Claude like a sounding board. For example, if I need 10 different hook ideas for a video, or I want to rewrite an email to sound a bit warmer and conversational, I'll use AI to get a quick draft. But I always edit it myself to make sure it sounds real and matches the brand's voice."

---

### 6. "How do you handle working independently and the US meeting overlap?"
> "I'm used to owning my own work. When you run a business, nobody is micromanaging you, so you have to be disciplined. I'm completely fine with the US Eastern overlap for team calls, syncs, and planning. Once I know what the priorities are, I just organize the tasks in Asana, get them done, and send a quick end-of-day summary so the team always knows where things stand."

---

### 7. "What do you do if you get stuck on a task or don't know how to use a tool?"
> "First, I'll try to figure it out on my own. I'll check tutorials, test things out, or look through the documentation. But if I'm still stuck after 20 or 30 minutes, I don't just sit on it quietly. I'll reach out to the team and say, 'Here's what I'm trying to do, here's what I tried, and here's what I think might work.' I always try to bring a couple of possible solutions instead of just dropping a problem in someone's lap."

---

### 8. "Can you tell me about a time a project didn't go as planned? How did you handle it?"
*(Shows extreme ownership—Tobe Brockner loves this)*
> "Early on with our cafe, we launched a promotional campaign and our delivery order sync glitched during a busy rush. It was completely on me for not testing the load properly. Instead of panicking or making excuses, I immediately jumped in to help the staff manually track orders, personally messaged the delayed customers, and then stayed up that evening fixing the system so it could handle high volume. The lesson for me was: always test systems thoroughly before going live, and always own the mistake right away."

---

### 9. "How do you handle feedback or multiple revisions on your videos and graphics?"
> "Honestly, I don't take creative feedback personally. Marketing isn't an art gallery—it's about getting results for the business. If the team or the client says a video pacing feels off or a graphic isn't hitting the brand tone, I listen closely, make the changes, and take note of their preferences so my next batch of content is closer to what they want on the first try."

---

### 10. "Why are you looking for a new role right now?"
> "Our cafe has systems in place now and runs smoothly day-to-day, so my hands-on operational time is much lighter. I really want to anchor myself with a solid, long-term remote company where I can dedicate my marketing, video, and systems skills to one brand and grow with them over time."

---

### 11. "What are your salary expectations?"
> "I'm flexible and mostly focused on finding the right long-term fit with the team. What is the approved salary range you have budgeted for this position?"  
> *(If they insist you name a number first):*  
> "Based on my experience running marketing for my own F&B business and my hands-on video editing skills, I'm looking around **$5 to $7 per hour** (or around **$800 to $1,100 per month**), but I'm very open to discussing what works best for your team."

---

## ❓ Great Questions to Ask Them at the End
*(Pick one or two when they ask: "Do you have any questions for us?")*

1. **"On the marketing side, what's the biggest headache or bottleneck right now? Is it keeping up with video content, or managing the email campaigns?"**
2. **"Does this wine client already have documented SOPs and brand guidelines in place, or will part of my role be helping build and organize those systems?"**
